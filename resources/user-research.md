# What Learners Want (and Struggle With)

Notes on beginner drawing tutorials, and the product decisions that follow.

---

## What beginners struggle with

- **Overwhelm / information overload.** There are endless tutorials, tools and
  techniques, and "learning to draw" feels like too much at once. Long, dense
  lessons lose people.
- **Fundamentals get skipped.** Beginners genuinely struggle with basics — drawing
  a decent circle, keeping a line straight, judging proportion. Tutorials that
  jump ahead leave them stuck.
- **Trying to draw the "whole thing" from memory** instead of observing, which
  produces distorted proportions (e.g. eyes placed too high on a face).
- **Fear of mistakes.** Frustration and self-judgement make people quit early.

## What beginners want

1. **Complexity broken into simple shapes and small steps** they can actually
   follow, with visible progress.
2. **Clear, step-by-step guidance** that doesn't skip the hard parts.
3. **Not too much at once** — manageable chunks, one idea per step.
4. **Encouragement** — permission to make mistakes and keep going.
5. **To see it done** — watching the marks appear, then copying, beats reading a
   paragraph.

---

## Design decisions that follow

| Insight | What we do in the app |
|---------|-----------------------|
| Break complexity into small pieces | Grid method: each step is one small cell, not "the face." |
| Big shapes before detail | A **block-in** phase draws the major outline first; detail comes later. |
| Correct proportions, don't guess | **Loomis proportion guides** for faces (eye line halfway down, etc.). |
| One idea per step, don't overwhelm | Steps grouped into named **phases** (Block-in → Guides → Detail → Shading), short plain-language text. |
| Show, don't just tell | Every step **animates** the marks with a pencil; a "Watch it drawn" mode plays the whole build. |
| The subject matters most | **Face-first ordering** + extra detail on the face; background is simplified. |
| Shading is a distinct skill, done last | A dedicated **shading phase**: squint, find darks, hatch light-to-dark. |
| Encouragement | Supportive copy ("take your time," "don't worry about detail yet," a celebratory finish). |
| Let people go at their own pace | Full sketch shown immediately; click any step to study it; play/pause; speed control. |
| Reduce friction & fear | 100% in-browser, nothing uploaded, free, with sample images to try instantly. |
